Boxim - team BlackSeal

Description of the project

Our project is a cartoon-style boxing simulation that blends virtual reality with real-world interaction. The player physically punches a real mattress, while their movements are tracked and translated into a virtual environment where their fists interact with a digital punching bag.

By combining VR immersion with tangible physical feedback, the game aims to offer the best of both worlds: the realism and engagement of real movements, and the creativity and visual freedom of a virtual environment. This mixed-reality approach creates an intuitive, fun, and immersive boxing experience that emphasizes interaction, accessibility, and player engagement.

Goal and Motivation

Our project’s main objective is to deepen our proficiency in Unity, particularly for creating immersive 3D experiences. We are eager to gain hands-on experience with virtual reality, exploring its potential to enhance user interaction and engagement. What particularly motivates us is experimenting with the combination of virtual and real-world interactions within a boxing experience: we aim to integrate users’ physical movements with digital and physical feedback to create an engaging simulation. At the same time, this project allows us to develop our creativity and ability to design innovative interactions, while strengthening our technical skills in programming, 3D design, and immersive ergonomics. Ultimately, we hope this work will give us a stronger understanding of the challenges and possibilities of mixed-reality environments.

Graphics and Interaction Technologies Used and Developed

For this project, we relied mostly on established software to create an immersive boxing experience. Unity served as the main development platform, allowing us to build and integrate the 3D environment, handle physics interactions, and manage VR functionalities. Blender was used to design and model the 3D assets, including the sandbag and the health bar, providing the visual detail necessary for a convincing experience

We also experimented with developing a punch detection system using the MediaPipe library in combination with a camera. This system was designed to track hand movements and detect hits on the virtual sandbag. However, during testing, we found that the Oculus Quest 2’s built-in detection features were more accurate and responsive, so we ultimately relied on the Quest 2 for hand tracking. The Quest 2’s hand detection allows precise tracking of individual fingers and hand positions, enabling natural and intuitive interactions without controllers. Additionally, we utilized the Quest 2’s passthrough functionality, which lets users see their real-world surroundings within VR. This feature helped us safely integrate physical movements into the virtual environment, bridging the gap between real and virtual interactions.

This combination of technologies allowed us to create a realistic, responsive, and safe mixed-reality boxing experience.

Challenges and Obstacles

One of the main challenges we faced was our limited experience with Unity. Most of the team had never worked with the platform before, which made it initially difficult to implement 3D models, physics, and VR interactions. Learning the engine while simultaneously developing the project required careful planning and a lot of experimentation, as we needed to balance learning with producing functional results.

Coordinating our work as a team was another significant obstacle. A project of this complexity required dividing tasks efficiently while ensuring that the various components—3D models, physics, UI, and VR interactions—remained compatible. Communication and version control were crucial to avoid conflicts and ensure smooth integration of everyone’s contributions.

Detecting punches accurately also presented a challenge. We initially attempted to use MediaPipe with an external camera to track hand movements and detect impacts on the sandbag. While the system worked in principle, it proved less reliable than the Quest 2’s native hand-tracking feature, which provided more precise detection of hand position and gestures. Switching to the Quest 2’s built-in solution ultimately improved responsiveness and made the experience more natural.

Creating realistic physics for the sandbag was another technical hurdle. The sandbag needed to react authentically to hits, with appropriate movement and feedback, without becoming unpredictable or breaking immersion. This required tuning physics parameters carefully and extensive testing to strike the right balance between realism and stability.

Finally, we had to consider the target audience for the museum exhibition, particularly children. Designing the sandbag to look engaging but not frightening required thoughtful artistic choices. We needed to ensure that the experience was exciting and immersive without creating elements that might appear too aggressive or scary for younger visitors.

Lessons learned

One of the key lessons we learned during this project is the critical importance of coordination and communication within a team. Clearly defining tasks, assigning roles, and maintaining an organized workflow are essential, especially for complex projects involving multiple components like 3D modeling, physics, and VR interactions. Using tools like GitHub effectively helped us track progress, avoid conflicts, and ensure that everyone’s work was integrated smoothly. We realized that consistent communication and planning significantly reduce confusion and make the development process much more efficient.

Another important lesson was to be confident in the project’s concept before diving into development. Early in the project, we frequently changed our approach, which caused delays and required reworking parts of the system. Initially, our plan focused on using only haptic feedback with a virtual sandbag that could provide tactile sensations to the player. Later, we modified the concept to include a real impact on a mattress combined with haptic feedback. Ultimately, we decided to abandon haptic feedback altogether. This experience taught us that solidifying the core idea before starting development can save time, avoid unnecessary complexity, and help the team focus on building a coherent and functional experience.
